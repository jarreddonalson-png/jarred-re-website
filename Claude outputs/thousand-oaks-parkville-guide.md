## Where Thousand Oaks Sits — and Why It Matters

Thousand Oaks is tucked into southern Platte County, straddling unincorporated county land and the city of Parkville depending on which phase you're looking at. The big geographic story here is the bluffs — homes in this community sit high above the Missouri River, which means views, mature tree lines, and the kind of natural backdrop that's genuinely hard to find this close to Kansas City.

You're roughly 20 minutes from the Power & Light District on a normal day. Parkville's Main Street is even closer — walkable coffee shops, local restaurants, the Missouri River waterfront, and Parkville's Friday Night Live events in the warmer months. It's one of the few spots north of the river where you get real neighborhood character without sacrificing convenience.

---

## What the Homes Look Like

Thousand Oaks is a newer-build community with multiple builders active across different phases — Inspired Homes, Roeser Homes, Rob Washam Homes, and Legacy Homes of Kansas City have all built here, so the product varies more than a single-builder neighborhood. What you'll consistently find are larger floor plans — mostly 4 and 5 bedroom homes, 1.5 to 2-story layouts, and 3-car garages built into the design from the start.

Price range has settled in the upper end of the north-of-the-river market. Depending on the builder and phase, you're generally looking at the upper $700s into the mid-$900s for new construction. Resale homes exist in the community as well, and they tend to sell quickly because demand for the area has stayed strong even as inventory has loosened elsewhere.

The architecture leans toward traditional with modern interior finishes — open-concept main floors, higher ceilings, primary suites with enough space to actually use. These aren't starter homes. They're built for people who've already done the starter-home chapter and are ready for a house that fits how they actually live.

---

## Amenities That Get Used

This is where Thousand Oaks earns its reputation. The community was designed with the idea that people would actually spend time in it, and the amenities back that up.

There are two swimming pools, a clubhouse (the Oasis Clubhouse, which is also available to rent for private events), tennis courts, and parks and playgrounds scattered through the development. The trail system is one of the things buyers mention most — an extensive network of walking and nature trails that winds through green spaces, around lakes, and into the nature reserve areas built into the community's footprint.

Beyond the physical amenities, there's an actual community culture here. The Otters Swim Team runs through the summer. Food Truck Fridays are a staple. There's a calendar of events that makes the HOA feel less like a rules organization and more like a neighborhood association in the real sense of the word.

If you have kids who will spend their summers outside, or you're someone who actually wants to know your neighbors, Thousand Oaks tends to deliver on that.

---

## Schools

Thousand Oaks falls within the Park Hill School District, which is one of the main draws for families moving to Parkville. Union Chapel Elementary and Lakeview Middle School serve the neighborhood, feeding into Park Hill South High School.

I'm not going to give you rankings — those change and they tell an incomplete story anyway. What I can tell you is that Park Hill is consistently cited by the families I work with as a major reason they chose this part of the metro. If schools are central to your decision, spend time on the district's website and visit if you can. The facts on the ground will tell you more than any number.

## What to Weigh Before You Decide

No neighborhood is the right fit for everyone, and I'd rather you know the trade-offs up front.

**Price point.** Thousand Oaks sits at the top of the north-of-the-river market. If you're stretching to get there, it's worth having an honest conversation about what you're giving up elsewhere in your budget — maintenance reserves, flexibility, the ability to do the updates you want. A home you can breathe in financially feels different from one you can't.

**Newness.** The community is still in active development in some phases, which means construction traffic and the reality of a neighborhood that isn't fully planted and settled yet. If you're buying new, factor that in.

**HOA structure.** There's an active HOA with covenants, architectural review, and a fine schedule. For some buyers, that's a feature — it protects the character of the neighborhood long-term. For others, it's friction. Know which one you are.

Thousand Oaks is one of the stronger new-build communities north of the river, and it holds its value for a reason. The combination of location, amenities, and school district makes it a legitimate long-term choice — not just a place to park money, but a place to actually live.
